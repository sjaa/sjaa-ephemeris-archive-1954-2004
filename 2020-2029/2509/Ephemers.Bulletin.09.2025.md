## PAH

Carbon, Hydrogen, and Oxygen make up 93% of the human body. As we learned from one of our most recent Astro 101 talks (Gravity, Rockets, Rogues and Aliens), these elements are among the most common in the universe. They exist in stars, in the interstellar medium (aka space dust), and in the gas that makes up some of the brightest deep sky objects that we love to observe. What, then, separates human beings from space dust?

Perhaps not as much as you may think. Scientists hypothesize that the same or similar hydrocarbons found throughout the galaxy were also key ingredients in the "primordial soup" from which organic life arose here on Earth. Specifically, Polycyclic Aromatic Hydrocarbons, or PAHs, are getting renewed attention, with the discovery of more and more of these complex molecules out in the cosmos. Our very own target of the month, the Iris Nebula, is suspected to contain such PAHs, the potential building blocks of life. Kind of makes me wish I had paid more attention in high school chemistry class. Kind of.

Don't forget to keep looking up!

## Target of the Month: Iris Nebula

The Iris Nebula, also known as Caldwell 4, NGC 7023, and LBN 487, is a bright reflection nebula in the constellation Cepheus. Situated about 3.5 degrees southwest of Beta Cep (Alfirk), the Iris is illuminated by the relatively young star named V380 Cep. The young star is surrounded by dust that reflects the nearby starlight in characteristic blue tones. Among the nebulosity is a star cluster that adds further interest to the view.

At a magnitude of somewhere between 6.8 and 7.2, the Iris is a difficult visual target in small scopes, but shines gloriously in photographs. A 10" scope should reveal bright visual views of the nebula, and good conditions can bring out the dark patches amidst the nebulosity. In a smart scope, or other long exposure photos, the bright blue color and endlessly intricate dark patches give the imagination a lot of fun shapes to play with.

From our vantage point on Earth, the nebula lies behind clouds of dust, which partly obscure it from view. This same dust also lends its own beauty to the image. These patches of obscuring dust even have their own name. So-called "dark nebulae" are often very rewarding targets in and of themselves. Many of which are catalogued in the Lynd's Catalog of Dark Nebulae (LDN). Lynd also has a catalogue of bright nebulae, LBN, of which the Iris is a proud member.
