# Member Programs

## SJAA LIBRARY

SJAA offers another wonderful resource: a library with good astronomy books and DVDs available to all of our members that will interest all age groups and especially young children who are budding astronomers! You may send all your questions/ comments to **librarian@sjaa.net**.

## TELESCOPE FIX-IT SESSION

Fix-It Day, sometimes called the Telescope Tune Up or the Telescope Fix-It program, is a really simple service the SJAA offers to members of the SJAA community for free, though it's priceless. Headed up by Larry Zurbrick, the Fix It session provides a place for people to come with their telescope or other astronomy gear problems and have them looked at, such as broken scopes whose owners need advice, or need help with collimating a telescope.

**http://www.sjaa.net/programs/fixit/**

## QUICK START PROGRAM

The Quick STARt Program, headed up by Jonathan Lawton, helps to ease folks into amateur astronomy. You have to admit, astronomy can look exciting from the outside, but once you scratch the surface, it can get seemingly complex in a hurry. But it doesn't have to be that way if there's someone to guide you and answer all your seemingly basic questions. The Quick STARt sessions are generally held quarterly.

**http://www.sjaa.net/programs/quick-start/**

## LOANER PROGRAM

Muditha Kanchana (Kanch) heads up this program. The Program goal is for SJAA members to be able to evaluate equipment they are considering purchasing or are just curious about by checking out loaners from SJAA's growing list of equipment. Please note that certain items have restrictions or special conditions that must be met.

If you are an SJAA member and an experienced observer or have been through the SJAA Quick STARt program please fill this form to request a particular item. Please also consider donating unused equipment.

**http://www.sjaa.net/programs/loaner-telescope-program/**

# ASTRO IMAGING SIG

SIG has a mission of bringing together people who have an interest in astronomy imaging, or put more simply, taking pictures of the night sky. Run by Hy Murveit, the Imaging SIG meets roughly every month over Zoom, often on the 3rd Tuesday evening of the month. We usually have a guest speaker presenting on some topic related to astro-imaging. Talks begin at 7:30pm. There is an opportunity to socialize and discuss imaging starting at 7pm when we start the Zoom call. Meeting topics, dates and times are posted on the SJAA Astro Imaging mail list in Google Groups. Past meetings are available on **YouTube**.

**http://www.sjaa.net/programs/imaging-sig/**

## ASTRO IMAGING WORKSHOPS AND FIELD CLINICS

Not to be confused with the Imaging SIG group, this newly organized program is a hands on program for club members, who are interested in astrophotography, to have a chance of seeing what it is all about.

Workshops are held at Houge Park once per month and field clinics (members only) once per quarter at a dark sky site. Keep an eye on the Google Groups and website for information about the next session.

# Public Programs

## ASTRONOMY 101

Astronomy 101 covers various topics related to beginner astronomy and star gazing. The popular Intro to the Night Sky session takes place monthly, in conjunction with first quarter moon and In Town Star Parties at Houge Park. This is a regular, monthly session, run by Carl Svensson. Content varies from "what's up in the night sky" to basics of star-gazing and telescopes to specific topics of interest. After the session, the attendees will go outside for a guided, green laser tour of the sky, along with views through SJAA Telescopes at the In-Town Star Party, to get a better look at the night's celestial objects.

**http://www.sjaa.net/programs/beginners-astronomy/**

# SCHOOL STAR PARTY

The San Jose Astronomical Association conducts evening observing sessions (commonly called "star parties") for schools in mid-Santa Clara County, generally from Sunnyvale to Fremont to Morgan Hill. Contact ssp@sjaa.net for additional information.

**http://www.sjaa.net/programs/school-star-party/**

## SOLAR OBSERVING

Solar observing sessions, headed up by Wolf Witt, are usually held the 1st Sunday of every Month from 2pm - 4pm at Houge Park weather permitting. Please check SJ Astronomy Meetup for schedule details as the event time and location is subject to change.

**http://www.meetup.com/SJ-Astronomy/**

## IN-TOWN STAR PARTY (ITSP)

This popular public star party is held every month, at our headquarters in Houge Park in San Jose. Enjoy celestial wonders from our very own backyard. Our volunteers are more than happy to show you the great views through their telescopes. Check our Meetup page for specific dates and times. All are welcome!

# BEGINNER ASTRONOMY FORUM

This exclusively online event is an opportunity for anyone to come and ask questions about the early stages of astronomy and star-gazing as a hobby. Unlike our other Astronomy 101 offers, this session is entirely guided by your questions, so come prepared! We will have two or three SJAA volunteers on-hand to share their knowledge.

## STARRY NIGHTS

SJAA partners with the Santa Clara County Open Space Authority (OSA) to co-host monthly public star parties, called Starry Nights. These events are held at Rancho Canada del Oro (RCDO) Open Space Preserve just 30 minutes south of downtown San Jose. Come enjoy a night sky that is sheltered from city lights. Space is limited, so reserve your spot at:

**http://www.meetup.com/SJ-Astronomy/**

## BINOCULAR STARGAZING

Want to learn the night sky? Did you know that you don't need to spend a ton of money on a telescope to do so? All you need is a decent pair of binoculars. Bring your 10x50mm binoculars, some comfy clothes, a chair, and join us on a tour of the night sky!

**http://www.sjaa.net/events/binocular-stargazing/**

# Yosemite Star Party

**By Carl Svensson**

This July, SJAA made a triumphant return to Glacier Point in Yosemite National Park for a two-night stint hosting the Stars Over Yosemite star party. This star party is hosted by a revolving cast of amateur astronomy clubs. Thanks to the diligent planning of Ken Miura and Rob Jaworski, we were able to join the ranks and hosted the well-attended event on the evenings of July 18th and 19th. Volunteers included Gary Mitchell, Shiva Koneti, Michael Madden, Carl Svensson, Rajah Chandrasekhar, Serena Li, Ken Miura, Brad Haakenson, and Mark Hanson.

We were fortunate enough to be granted access to several camp sites for the 17th through 20th, and free admission to the park. Armed with detailed directions by Gary and Jim Van Nuland from years past, I arrived at the Bridalveil Creek campsite around 4pm. A few others were already setting up their tents, and after taking care of the arrival logistics, we got to enjoy some personal observing time at the campground ahead of the busy nights to come. Mike and I ventured up a rocky path to a nearby plateau with our binoculars. My binocular stargazing gained at least two levels as Mike patiently showed me how to find such crowd-pleasing gems as the Lagoon, Trifid, Eagle Nebula, and all the other fuzzies that run up the band of the Milky Way from the summer's low southern horizon. It was the perfect preparation for the upcoming star parties. Our session was cut short by a mountain lion who walked by us, not twenty feet away. It casually looked at us as if strode by, and we took that as our cue to head back to camp!

The next morning, Brad, Mike, and I hit the road in Brad's Jeep to explore the valley in the light of day. I expected much more traffic for a Friday in the summer, but luckily we were able to get around with no delays, and make use of Brad's "parking Karma" to find us good parking spots in the nearly full lots. The only traffic jam we encountered was a line of stopped cars on the way into the valley. Poking our heads out of the car, we could see a black bear had crossed the road, and was meandering through the nearby hills. All cameras were out in full force for 15-20 minutes, and then we were on our way.

Driving through the park, or better yet, being *driven* through the park is a breath taking experience in and of itself. Every turn reveals a new, majestic vista featuring Half Dome, El Cap, or some other less famous, but equally stunning geography. Dozens of domes, forests, valleys, and waterfalls leap out at you, and beg for your admiration. Of course, we obliged, and got our necessary selfies and panoramas out of the way early. Brad and Mike helped me recreate a picture of me at the tunnel vista, sitting on a ledge overlooking Half Dome. A four-year-old version of me posed in that same position 35 years earlier, as my parents dutifully reminded me.

Upon arriving at the visitor's center in the valley, we sought out some very helpful rangers at the information kiosk near the very full parking lot. Laura and Beth kindly annotated some maps for us, tailoring the sights to our schedule. We told them about our scheduled star party, and they were very eager to learn more, and even attend themselves. # Observing Highlights

## 09/21
**Saturn at Opposition**
The ringed planet will be at its closest approach to Earth and its face will be fully illuminated by the Sun.

## 09/22
**September Equinox**
The September equinox occurs at 18:17 UTC. This is the first day of fall in the Northern Hemisphere.

## 10/21
**Orionids Meteor Shower**
With an average of 20 meteors per hour, and no moon in sight, this should be a great year for the Orionids.

## 10/29
**Mercury at Greatest Elongation**
Mercury reaches its furthest point from the Sun at 23.9 degrees. Look for it low in the western sky just after sunset.

## 11/05
**Full Supermoon**
This "Beaver Moon" is also the second of three supermoons in 2025, where it will appear larger and brighter.

## 11/17
**Leonids Meteor Shower**
With only a thin, crescent moon, the Leonids should put on a good show this year.

A quick hike to the Lower Yosemite Fall, and lunch in the valley left us ready for the first star party.

After most of the remaining volunteer-campers arrived and set up camp, we set out from Bridalveil Drop at Glacier Point. We were met by the kind and helpful Ranger Diana, our liaison to the park, who directed our cars to a dirt path off the main parking lot that dropped us right near the amphitheater for loading and unloading. Ken's minivan got stuck in the dirt, but I used a nearby branch to dig under the stuck tire, and provide traction for the wheel to turn. I had my Celestron NexStar 8SE, Analog Sky Heart 80mm binoculars, and an all-sky time-lapse camera set up near the ledge on the bottom level of the amphitheater. Ken and Mark set up at the top of the amphitheater; Ken with his Takahashi refractor and Mark with his gigantic Dob. Rajah, Mike, and later Shiva had their Dobs on the bottom level. Smart scopes from Brad and Serena and Gary's SCT rounded out the offerings.

Clouds threatened in the evening twilight, and early visitors were pleased with views of Half Dome from my binoculars. We even saw some hikers on the top of Half Dome! The clouds gave way to clear skies within an hour or so after sunset, but there was still enough clearing to show sights until then. Most attendees I interacted with were completely new to astronomy and stargazing, so it was fun to show them around the naked eye, binocular, and telescopic sights. The Milky Way and Andromeda were crowd favorites, but the Dumbbell, Bode's Galaxy, and the Lagoon also shone bright under the Bortle 2 skies and 7,000 foot elevation of Glacier Point. I spoke with two individuals whose sole goal was to see the Milky Way: an Indian ex-pat living in Dubai, and a guy from Jersey. Helping my new friend from Jersey capture the Milky Way through his camera phone and DSLR was a real treat. He had crossed the country for this experience, and we helped him get some great images, under some of the best skies in the world. Words simply cannot describe the feeling.

If Friday night was a success, then Saturday night was a triumph. We arrived in the same fashion as Friday night, fending off a couple of confused visitors who had followed our cars down the dirt path. Fueled by Shiva's worldfamous Chai tea, we set up our equipment amidst a crowd of about 50 or so people who lined the amphitheater waiting for us to begin. Our Ranger friends, Laura and Beth, had spread the word to 1300 people, and as sunset drew closer, more people arrived, anxious to start seeing the night sky. With such a crowd ready to go, I was able to give a short introduction to star party etiquette. An enthusiastic cheer from the crowd confirmed that they were here to see some stars, and the floodgates opened. The crowd was indeed heaviest in that first hour, as those who had been eagerly waiting transitioned to eagerly standing in line at our various stations. We maintained a steady crowd through midnight, but at a much more manageable pace. It was just the right amount of chaos to give a fun energy to the start of our final night in Yosemite.

The following report from Mike captures that energy, and some very special highlights:

*The most gratifying for me was hearing the excitement from the guests who travelled to Yosemite from all corners of the world. When I traced the summer triangle and pointed to Vega with the laser pointer, a visitor from Korea said, "Really?! That's my name! Can you put it in the telescope?" I slewed it over to Vega and she gasped at seeing her namesake. After hearing her excitement, the others in the line wanted me to keep it on Vega rather than move it back to the Hercules Cluster.*

*I traced out Scorpius to a family from England who said they can't see the bottom of the constellation from 52N where they live and had never seen it as a scorpion until now. There were a few multi-generational families who came by - parents translating to the grandparents and to the kids to tell them what to look for in the eyepiece. A visitor from Dubai who grew up in the southern hemisphere stood in amazement looking to the north and seeing parts of the sky he never knew.*

*A group from Los Angeles came by later on Saturday and had so many interesting questions. They came late enough to see Saturn rising and witness the almostlinear rings and Neptune less than a degree away. As we talked about how so few of us can see the Milky Way where we live, I recounted the (maybe apocryphal) story about how people in LA during the power outage from the 1994 Northridge earthquake called 911 to report a long glowing line in the sky and asked if it was from the earthquake.*

*Some sky highlights for me were seeing the Triangulum galaxy with direct vision once it got high enough, seeing lots of globulars with the naked eye, and glimpsing several Alpha Capricornid meteor trails. Rajah showed us the Veil Nebula in his massive Dobsonian, the best I've ever seen. The moonrise on Saturday was one for the books. As the moon rose from behind Half Dome, we first saw the earthglow side of the moon in detail. A few seconds later, the corners of the sunlit side poked out from behind Half Dome like little horns. As the moon got higher, it basked us and the valley below with bright white moonlight. For me, this rare sight was up there with a total solar eclipse.*

Indeed, the moonrise over Half Dome, followed shortly thereafter by the Pleiades was the icing on the cake. It was such a beautiful sight in the calm that followed an energetic star party, set in the crisp air, surrounded by the splendor of Yosemite's unparalleled geography, and among friends. On Sunday morning, we were treated to round two of Shiva's Chai and accompanying snacks.

Taken altogether, the star parties and the adventure of exploring the park made this an outstanding experience. We shared our passion for the cosmos with many people from all over the world. We had daytime excitement on the hiking trails, where we only got a little bit lost. We had wildlife encounters. We even had a minor altercation between a car and a boulder. We had great company and even better Chai. Most importantly, we had the pristine skies of Yosemite, and we had the stars.

# The View from Below the Skies

**LAST MAY** at the Lick Observatory volunteer orientation night I had the pleasure to reacquaint and meet with other long term and new Lick Observatory volunteers. One special attendee was Lisa Pappanpostos, Senior Ranger for Joseph D. Grant County Park on Mt. Hamilton Road, located several miles below Lick Observatory in Halls Valley. Lisa announced that she was seeking volunteers help to organize a public star party at Grant Ranch on August 16. I offered to help with organizing a few volunteers.

When the date of August 16 arrived, the weather forecast did not look very good and two other SJAA events that were scheduled for the same evening were cancelled. However, we pressed on and took our chances, usually observing through openings in the clouds during the early evening. The situation improved a bit as the evening progressed. The Park staff had organized an interpretive program for the public. Lisa reported that attendance was 182 people. Many thanks to SJAA volunteers John Gates, Mark Hanson, Steve Elstad, Rich Gregor, Murali Balasubramnaiam, and Stan Webster (see image on the facing page).

We had a combination of SCT's, a Dob, a refractor, and Seestar scopes to show the public. We had 5 to 6 people lined up at the 'scopes for most of the event. The Seestars captured images of the Eastern Veil Nebula, M51, M31, and M27. Lisa and other Park staff declared the star party a success. It was the first star party at Grant since the monthly star parties ended at the beginning of COVID in 2020. The Park is planning on organizing ½- or 4-star parties in 2026. Stay tuned for future volunteer requests!

**MORE NEWS** about Grant Ranch County Park. During the same conversation in May with Lisa Pappanastos at Lick Observatory, I mentioned that SJAA had a county park permit to observe at Mendoza Ranch. Lisa suggested that we add Grant Ranch to our existing permit. We have now added Grant Ranch to that permit to allow for SJAA member-only observing at Grant Ranch. I am currently recruiting SJAA members to help coordinate observing activities at Grant Ranch in much the same manner as we do for Mendoza Ranch and RCDO. If you are interested in helping with this, please send me an email at president@sjaa.net. Observing dates will be announced soon. Ad astra.

**Larry Zurbrick**

# Summer Nights in The Sierras – Or – The Night I Counted Past One Hundred
## By Marianne Damon

Out onto the porch we pull our mattresses, and the big, billowy comforters, too warm for indoor use.

Snuggled under, we watch the sky. First, the twilight bats, darting across the silhouettes of pines.

Ah! There's Venus, first light in the greying sky. Slowly, twilight descends into lovely darkness.

"I call first!" A faint star trail is fleetingly seen.

"Oh, oh, OH, did you see THAT?" A huge bolide lights up the sky. "That's my number 42!"

By 75 my sisters have stopped counting, their faces under the comforter away from the cold and mosquitoes.

There's my favorite tiny constellation, the Dolphin!

106...107...108...and my dreams are floating gently on the sea of stars.

![Photo of two people holding a certificate]

SJAA celebrated the retirement from OSA of our liaison Teri Rogoway at a potluck picnic at Alum Rock Park. On the eve of June 28, over forty OSA staff, docents and friends gathered to share delicious food and fun stories about Teri's decades-long tenure at the Open Space Authority.

Six of us from SJAA presented Teri with an anniversary SJAA tote bag, a constellation mug, and a certificate of appreciation. We talked about our "first contact", which was when Jim Van Nuland asked Teri if she could use some help setting up a telescope. The answer was an immediate, enthusiastic, "Yes, PLEASE!" Joe Fragola and Brad Haskenson then left to help support the Mendoza event. Marianne Damon and Nancy Lieu had delisted themselves from the Pinnacles volunteers in order to say farewell to Teri on behalf of SJAA.

Carl Wong was also there to thank Teri for her over-a-decade collaboration with us for programs including Starry Nights, Binocular Star Gazing, meteor shower events, Tilton Ranch events, and the use of Little Uvas. We wish Teri the best in her retirement and will always remember her as a true friend to SJAA.
