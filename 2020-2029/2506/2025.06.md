# Member Programs

## SJAA LIBRARY

SJAA offers another wonderful resource: a library with good astronomy books and DVDs available to all of our members that will interest all age groups and especially young children who are budding astronomers! You may send all your questions/comments to **librarian@sjaa.net**.

## TELESCOPE FIX-IT SESSION

Fix-It Day, sometimes called the Telescope Tune Up or the Telescope Fix-It program, is a really simple service the SJAA offers to members of the SJAA community for free, though it's priceless. Headed up by Larry Zurbrick, the Fix It session provides a place for people to come with their telescope or other astronomy gear problems and have them looked at, such as broken scopes whose owners need advice, or need help with collimating a telescope.

**http://www.sjaa.net/programs/fixit/**

## QUICK START PROGRAM

The Quick STARt Program, headed up by Jonathan Lawton, helps to ease folks into amateur astronomy. You have to admit, astronomy can look exciting from the outside, but once you scratch the surface, it can get seemingly complex in a hurry. But it doesn't have to be that way if there's someone to guide you and answer all your seemingly basic questions.

The Quick STARt sessions are generally held quarterly. The Quick STARt program has resumed under its new lead, Jonathon Lawton.

**http://www.sjaa.net/programs/quick-start/**

## LOANER PROGRAM

Muditha Kanchana (Kanch) heads up this program. The Program goal is for SJAA members to be able to evaluate equipment they are considering purchasing or are just curious about by checking out loaners from SJAA's growing list of equipment. Please note that certain items have restrictions or special conditions that must be met.

If you are an SJAA member and an experienced observer or have been through the SJAA Quick STARt program please fill this form to request a particular item. Please also consider donating unused equipment.

**http://www.sjaa.net/programs/loaner-telescope-program/**

## ASTRO IMAGING SIG

SIG has a mission of bringing together people who have an interest in astronomy imaging, or put more simply, taking pictures of the night sky. Run by Hy Murveit, the Imaging SIG meets roughly every month over Zoom, often on the 3rd Tuesday evening of the month. We usually have a guest speaker presenting on some topic related to astro-imaging. Talks begin at 7:30pm. There is an opportunity to socialize and discuss imaging starting at 7pm when we start the Zoom call. Meeting topics, dates and times are posted on the SJAA Astro Imaging mail list in Google Groups. Past meetings are available on **YouTube**.

**http://www.sjaa.net/programs/imaging-sig/**

## ASTRO IMAGING WORKSHOPS AND FIELD CLINICS

Not to be confused with the Imaging SIG group, this newly organized program is a hands on program for club members, who are interested in astrophotography, to have a chance of seeing what it is all about.

Workshops are held at Houge Park once per month and field clinics (members only) once per quarter at a dark sky site. Keep an eye on the Google Groups and website for information about the next session.

# Public Programs

## ASTRONOMY 101

Astronomy 101 covers various topics related to beginner astronomy and star gazing. The popular Intro to the Night Sky session takes place monthly, in conjunction with first quarter moon and In Town Star Parties at Houge Park. This is a regular, monthly session, run by Carl Svensson. Content varies from "what's up in the night sky" to basics of star-gazing and telescopes to specific topics of interest. After the session, the attendees will go outside for a guided, green laser tour of the sky, along with views through SJAA Telescopes at the In-Town Star Party, to get a better look at the night's celestial objects.

**http://www.sjaa.net/programs/beginners-astronomy/**

# SCHOOL STAR PARTY

The San Jose Astronomical Association conducts evening observing sessions (commonly called "star parties") for schools in mid-Santa Clara County, generally from Sunnyvale to Fremont to Morgan Hill. Contact ssp@sjaa.net for additional information.

**http://www.sjaa.net/programs/school-star-party/**

## SOLAR OBSERVING

Solar observing sessions, headed up by Wolf Witt, are usually held the 1st Sunday of every Month from 2pm - 4pm at Houge Park weather permitting. Please check SJ Astronomy Meetup for schedule details as the event time and location is subject to change.

**http://www.meetup.com/SJ-Astronomy/**

## IN-TOWN STAR PARTY (ITSP)

This popular public star party is held every month, at our headquarters in Houge Park in San Jose. Enjoy celestial wonders from our very own backyard. Our volunteers are more than happy to show you the grate views through their telescopes. Check our Meetup page for specific dates and times. All are welcome!

# BEGINNER ASTRONOMY FORUM

This exclusively online event is an opportunity for anyone to come and ask questions about the early stages of astronomy and star-gazing as a hobby. Unlike our other Astronomy 101 offers, this session is entirely guided by your questions, so come prepared! We will have two or three SJAA volunteers on-hand to share their knowledge.

## STARRY NIGHTS

SJAA partners with the Santa Clara County Open Space Authority (OSA) to co-host monthly public star parties, called Starry Nights. These events are held at Rancho Canada del Oro (RCDO) Open Space Preserve just 30 minutes south of downtown San Jose. Come enjoy a night sky that is sheltered from city lights. Space is limited, so reserve your spot at:

**http://www.meetup.com/SJ-Astronomy/**

## BINOCULAR STARGAZING

Want to learn the night sky? Did you know that you don't need to spend a ton of money on a telescope to do so? All you need is a decent pair of binoculars. Bring your 10x50mm binoculars, some comfy clothes, a chair, and join us on a tour of the night sky!

**http://www.sjaa.net/events/binocular-stargazing/**

# Seasons of the Solar System

---

*Kat Troche of the Astronomical Society of the Pacific via NASA's Night Sky Network*

Here on Earth, we undergo a changing of seasons every three months. But what about the rest of the Solar System? What does a sunny day on Mars look like? How long would a winter on Neptune be? Let's take a tour of some other planets and ask ourselves what seasons might look like there.

## MARTIAN AUTUMN

Although Mars and Earth have nearly identical axial tilts, a year on Mars lasts 687 Earth days (nearly 2 Earth years) due to its average distance of 142 million miles from the Sun, making it late autumn on the red planet. This distance and a thin atmosphere make it less than perfect sweater weather. A recent weather report from Gale Crater boasted a high of -18 degrees Fahrenheit for the week of May 20, 2025.

A diagram of Mars' orbit around the Sun, labeled with seasons for both hemispheres (Northern Spring/Southern Autumn, Northern Winter/Southern Summer, etc.). Key points like perihelion (closest to Sun) and aphelion (farthest from Sun) are marked, illustrating how seasons align with orbital position.

An artist's rendition of Mars' orbit around the Sun, and its seasons. Credit: NASA/JPL-Caltech

## SEVEN YEARS OF SUMMER

Saturn has a 27-degree tilt, very similar to the 25-degree tilt of Mars and the 23-degree tilt of Earth. But that is

where the similarities end. With a 29-year orbit, a single season on the ringed planet lasts seven years. While we can't experience a Saturnian season, we can observe a ring plane crossing here on Earth instead. The most recent plane crossing took place in March 2025, allowing us to see Saturn's rings 'disappear' from view.

## A LIFETIME OF SPRING

An image showing seasonal changes in Neptune between 1996 and 2002. The planet appears as a blue sphere with faint green and white atmospheric bands.

NASA Hubble Space Telescope observations in August 2002 show that Neptune's brightness has increased significantly since 1996. The rise is due to an increase in the amount of clouds observed in the planet's southern hemisphere. Credit: NASA, L. Sromovsky, and P. Fry (University of Wisconsin-Madison)

Even further away from the Sun, each season on Neptune lasts over 40 years. Although changes are slower and less dramatic than on Earth, scientists have observed seasonal activity in Neptune's atmosphere. These images were taken between 1996 and 2002 with the Hubble Space Telescope, with brightness in the southern hemisphere indicating seasonal change.

As we welcome summer here on Earth, you can build a Suntrack model (https://solar-center.stanford.edu/AO/Sun-Track-Model.pdf) that helps demonstrate the path the Sun takes through the sky during the seasons. You can find even more fun activities and resources like this model on NASA's Wavelength and Energy activity.

An average shower that can produce up to 20 meteors per hour at its peak, produced by cometary debris.

# Summer Space and Science

*Member and contributor, L.G. Boyll, has a list of great science and space-themed summer adventures for you to check out.*

## CALIFORNIA ACADEMY OF SCIENCES - SAN FRANCISCO, CA

https://www.calacademy.org/

With a natural history museum, aquarium, multi-story rainforest, and planetarium, you won't run out of things to check out at the Cal Academy of Science. The Morrison Planetarium hosts a 75-foot dome that will immerse you inside cutting edge visualizations and hyper-realistic virtual environments. "Incoming!" which is narrated by George Takei takes you up close with asteroids and comets while exploring the past, present and future of our Solar System. For adults, you can check out their Nightlife (advance tickets required) that happens on Thursdays and hosts a new theme with interactive exhibits, cocktails and music.

## CHABOT SPACE & SCIENCE CENTER - OAKLAND, CA

https://chabotspace.org/

Located in the picturesque Redwood Regional Park of Oakland, the Chabot Space & Science Center is a nonprofit science center that is home to the NASA Ames Visitor Center. It hosts an observatory, planetarium, exhibition galleries and more! Current exhibitions explore the mysteries of light and the Sun. You can pilot a rover mission control, see an authentic 7K-OK Russian Soyuz Descent Module, and even build your own test design to see how it stands up to other worlds. You can also register for summer events like family nature adventures or a celestial-themed paint and sip.

And if you can't make the trek to Oakland, you can still check out the Ames Gift Shop in Silicon Valley in Mountain View which has unique NASA gifts and official NASA gear.

## CHILDREN'S DISCOVERY MUSEUM OF SAN JOSE - SAN JOSE, CA

https://www.cdm.org

Explore, test, tinker, and create with over 150 interactive exhibits and programs in the sciences, humanities, arts, nature, and health. This award-winning museum sparks learning through play and provides endless hours of family fun, whether you're creating a ginormous bubble, manipulating objects with air pressure, igniting scientific inquiry, or splashing together while sending a ball soaring atop a jet of water.

## FREMONT PEAK OBSERVATORY ASSOCIATION - SAN JUAN BAUTISTA, CA

https://fpoa.net/

Located in Fremont Peak State Park near San Juan Bautista, Fremont Peak Observatory offers evening public programs that include an informative media presentation by a volunteer interpreter in the observatory classroom. When dark enough and weather permitting, the public is invited to observe with the 30" telescope and other telescopes until at least midnight. Daytime solar programs run from 2 to 5 PM and use special filters that allow you to safely view details on the sun's surface.

## LICK OBSERVATORY - MT. HAMILTON, CA

https://www.lickobservatory.org/

If you haven't yet had the pleasure of visiting, you're probably still familiar with seeing the prominent white buildings that sit atop Mt. Hamilton. At 4,200 feet and only 20 miles from San José, the historic Lick Observatory is an iconic part of our skyline. A photogenic spot - it's a great destination for a day or night time visit to learn more about Lick Observatory's research and instruments. No reservations are needed for the free weekend talks inside the dome of the historic 36-inch Great Refractor! You can also check out their visitor center and gift shop to learn more about their history and astronomy-themed merchandise. Or register ahead for one of their many other events that include public evening tours, concerts with astronomy lectures, and photography nights! Make sure you allow at least one hour from San José. The road up to the Observatory is scenic but winding!

## THE TECH INTERACTIVE - SAN JOSÉ, CA

https://www.thetech.org/

The Tech Interactive's galleries, which offer hands-on opportunities to explore how technological innovation is changing our lives and helping us tackle big challenges. Whether you have an hour or a full day, our exhibits will inspire, educate, and entertain. Make sure to check out the Space Exploration exhibit where you can maneuver a jet pack chair to fix a satellite, explore a futuristic Martian habitat, control a rover, conduct a scientific mission and even touch a piece of Mars!

# The View from Below the Skies

**STAR PARTIES HAVE EVOLVED** since I began attending (and volunteering at) so many decades ago. Amateur astronomers that shared their telescopes with the public in the distant past pointed their instruments mostly by manual means. Star hopping and star charts were the "de rigueur" of the day. Some telescope operators had encoders and a connected microcontroller database setup that allowed them to "push to" a given celestial object. I was always impressed by my fellow amateur astronomers that could star hop to any Messier or NGC object in much less than a minute and just explain that it was no big deal. They would tell me you just have to look for this so and so asterism and move two and a half fields of view in such and such direction! It always worked in relatively dark sky locations. Being able to look through the eyepiece, seeing a dim object with averted vision (averted imagination?!) and realizing what one was observing was enough for most people. I only heard, "Oh, another dim fuzzy" occasionally!

But times have changed. Most telescopes that I see at star parties are automated in that they are attached to go to mounts. Just enter the object you want to observe and wait

a few seconds. Not necessarily a bad thing since the public can see more objects and spend less time waiting for the telescope to be pointed. More time is spent explaining to the public what they are observing. I still hear the "Oh wow" from folks visually observing planets like Jupiter and Saturn and the brighter Messier objects. Interacting with the public is why many of us enjoy volunteering at star parties.

Visual astronomy now appears to be going by the wayside at star parties with the advent of Electronically Assisted Astronomy (EAA) telescopes. No longer do we need to explain why the view through the eyepiece does not appear like a Hubble telescope image. It is not because there is no eyepiece in the case of EAA telescopes. We can show dimmer objects on a tablet thanks to automatic image stacking. The public can see color and much more detail in the images and Bortle 8 light polluted skies are less of an issue. Thankfully our hobby has evolved and we can share more interesting celestial wonders with the public! No more "Oh, another dim fuzzy" hopefully will be uttered. Ad astra.

**Larry Zurbrik**  
*President, SJAA*

# How To Get Involved and Stay in Touch

Did you know that SJAA is on Social Media!? Of course we are. We don't live under a rock, afterall. If you just can't get enough astronomy, consider following us on any one of our social media platforms:

• X (Twitter) @sj_astronomy
• Facebook @sjastronomy
• Instagram @sjastronomy
• Blue Sky @sjastronomy
• Astrodon @sjastronomy

Not a fan of social media? No problem. Your SJAA membership gives you access to several important Google Groups. These groups serve both as forums and email lists, and are vital for club communications. Every member is added to the members@sjaa.net group, where SJAA leadership will make announcements about timely or important events. This group is read-only for most members. If you are a member and aren't getting messages, reach out to us for help at AskSJAA@sjaa.net.

Two other Google Groups are vital to round out your membership experience: sjaa-observers@googlegroups.com and sjaa-astroimaging@googlegroups.com. The observers group is great for connecting with other members about current astro events and member-only observing opportunities. The imaging group is for all our members with special interest in and astroimaging. Share your photos and get help from expert astrophotographers amongst our ranks. Both of these forums are accessible from groups.google.com as well as via e-mail. We encourage all members to read and post to these groups!

And then there's Meetup. Love it or hate it, SJAA has a robust presence on Meetup. This is where we post all of our public events.
