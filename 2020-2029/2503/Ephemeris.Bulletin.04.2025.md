## April Showers

As the saying goes, April showers bring May flowers. This month, April showers also bring *meteors* to our night skies in the form of the Lyrid meteor shower!

The Lyrid meteors are comet particles that are shed from the comet C/ 1861 G1 Thatcher. These meteors appear to radiate outwards from their namesake constellation, Lyra. Traveling at a speed of up to 29 miles per second, these bursts of light zip through the night sky pretty quickly, so be sure that you pick a dark spot and give your eyes about 30 minutes away from any bright lights to get fully dark-adapted. Bring some warm clothes, blankets and drinks and enjoy one of the oldest known meteor showers, first observed over 2,700 years ago.

April also brings plenty of great SJAA events, like Astro 101 talks and online Beginners' Forum, as well as ITSP, Starry Nights, and a Pinnacles Star Party! Keep looking up!

## Target of the Month - Leo Triplet
**by Joe Fragola**

Messier 65 and Messier 66 were discovered by Charles Messier on March 1, 1780, and NGC 3628 was discovered by William Herschel on April 8, 1784. The three galaxies interact with one another, each having an affect on the structure of the neighboring galaxies. The disk of M65 appears slightly warped, and a recent burst of star formation also indicates possible external disturbance from the other two galaxies. M66 shows asymmetrical spiral arms and a core that appears to be off center. NGC 3628 has a tidal tail approximately 300,000 light years long. Due to its shape resembling a hamburger, NGC 3628 has been nicknamed the "Hamburger Galaxy".

## Volunteer Opportunities

There are many volunteer opportunities in SJAA! While we love having seasoned experts in our volunteer ranks, we happily welcome enthusiastic beginners, too. Please do not hesitate to reach out to the volunteer coordinator (volunteerchair@sjaa.net) to inquire about opportunities. Below are a few examples of programs that could use help:

**Imaging SIG** The SJAA Imaging SIG (Special Interest Group) welcomes members to give talks in our monthly imaging meetings on topics related to astro-imaging. World-class expertise not required! Example topics include: comet astrophotography, solar imaging, Pixinsight skills, NINA tutorial, remote imaging, collimating telescopes, "my favorite astro gadgets", the science behind astrophotographs, "I love my mount/ scope/software/...". Please don't be modest, we need you! For past talks see our YouTube channel. Please email imaging.sig@sjaa.net if you're interested.

**Loaner Program** Our loaner program is looking for one to two more volunteers to help members check out gear and to demonstrate the set-up and usage of loaned items.
