## Galaxy Season

What's your favorite season? Spring? Summer? How about, Galaxy? For me, "Galaxy Season" is the best of them all. While perhaps a bit more conceptual than its traditional counterparts, Galaxy Season is the time of year, typically throughout the spring and summer in the northern hemisphere, when constellations rich in galaxies are best placed for nighttime observing.

Highlights include the Leo Triplet, M81, M82, the Whirlpool Galaxy, and this month's feature, the Sombrero Galaxy. The Sombrero is found in Virgo, which also hosts Markarian's Chain: an amazing stretch of galaxies that form a long chain celestial wonder set against a backdrop of stars and other distant galaxies.

May is also another month full of great SJAA events, including another Pinnacles Star Party on the 24th, and our highly acclaimed Telescope Workshop on the 18th. Don't miss out, and keep looking up!

## Target of the Month - M104

**by Joe Fragola**

Also known as the "Sombrero Galaxy," M104 was discovered by Charles Messier's colleague Pierre Méchain on May 11, 1781. Charles Messier made a handwritten note about M104 and added it to his personal list, but it was not officially included in the Messier Catalog until 1921, when the French astronomer, and author, Camille Flammarion found said list.

The nickname for M104 comes from the appearance of the central galactic bulge and the dust lane that crosses in front of it. The symmetrical dust ring around the bulge contains most of M104's hydrogen gas and dust. It is the primary site of starburst activity within the galaxy. Messier 104 also boasts a large population of globular clusters. Estimates range from 1,200 to 2,000 globular clusters. By contrast, our own Milky Way galaxy has only 150 known globular clusters.

Find the Sombrero Galaxy in the constellation Virgo. Look for an object about 8.0 magnitude with an apparent size of about 9 x 4'. Happy hunting!

## Volunteer Opportunities

There are many volunteer opportunities in SJAA! While we love having seasoned experts in our volunteer ranks, we happily welcome enthusiastic beginners, too. Please do not hesitate to reach out to the volunteer coordinator ([volunteerchair@sjaa.net](mailto:volunteerchair@sjaa.net)) to inquire about opportunities. Below are a few examples of programs that could use help:

**Imaging SIG** The SJAA Imaging SIG (Special Interest Group) welcomes members to give talks in our monthly imaging meetings on topics related to astro-imaging. World-class expertise not required! Example topics include: comet astrophotography, solar imaging, Pixinsight skills, NINA tutorial, remote imaging, collimating telescopes, "my favorite astro gadgets", the science behind astrophotographs, "I love my mount/ scope/software/...". Please don't be modest, we need you! For past talks see our YouTube channel ([@sjaa-astronomy74972](https://www.youtube.com/@sjaa-astronomy74972)).
